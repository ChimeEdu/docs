Timelines give students a visual, explorable sequence of events that turns a list of dates into a genuine narrative. Add events in any order — ChimeEdu sorts them chronologically for you automatically — then publish to your classroom and let students zoom, scrub, and tap their way through the content at their own pace.

## Creating a Timeline

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New Activity">
    From your teacher dashboard, click **New Activity** and select **Timeline**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your events">
    Click **Add Event** to start building your timeline. Add events in any order — there is no need to enter them chronologically. ChimeEdu will sort them for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in each event card">
    For every event, provide:

    - **Title** — the name of the event
    - **Date** — a specific date or year
    - **Image** — a photograph, illustration, or diagram (optional but highly recommended)
    - **Description** — formatted rich text giving students the context they need
    - **Location** — an optional place name or geographic reference
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the timeline">
    The classroom is chosen from a dropdown when you create the activity — there is no separate publish step, and no draft state. The timeline is visible to that classroom from the moment it is created, and edits go live as you save them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Build the deck in any order that suits your workflow — look up one event at a time, paste in notes as you go, or bulk-add events and fill in descriptions later. The timeline will always sort events by date automatically when students open it.
</Tip>

## Play Mode

Play Mode lets you present the timeline to the whole class in a guided, auto-advance format — perfect for lessons where you want to walk students through events in sequence rather than letting them explore independently.

To activate Play Mode:

1. Open the published timeline from your dashboard.
2. Click the **Play** button in the activity toolbar.
3. Events reveal one at a time in chronological order, advancing automatically or on your cue.

Use Play Mode at the start of a new topic to introduce the sequence before students explore on their own, or at the end of a unit as a review walkthrough. You can pause at any event to discuss it with the class before moving on.

<Note>
  You can attach Google Drive files — documents, slides, PDFs — to the timeline activity on **Plus and Premium** plans. Attachments belong to the activity as a whole and appear on its landing page, so they can't be pinned to one specific event. If a source relates to a particular moment, say so in that event's description.
</Note>

## Let students build their own timeline

On Plus and Premium you can issue a timeline **per student**, so each one builds their own sequence of events rather than everyone editing the same timeline. Events you added before issuing it stay visible to everyone as a shared starting layer, and a student's PDF export contains their own work alongside it. See [Per-Student & Groups](/teachers/activities/issuance).

## Subject Ideas

Timelines are most obviously associated with history, but the format works anywhere sequence matters:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="History">
    - Trace the rise and fall of ancient civilisations, mapping key turning points along the way.
    - Chart the sequence of battles in a military campaign.
    - Build a political timeline of a revolution from first cause to resolution.
    - Map a century of social change through legislation, protest, and reform.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Literature">
    - Plot a character's arc through a novel, marking key moments of growth or conflict.
    - Build a timeline of events within a story to help students track narrative structure.
    - Map the publication history of a writer's body of work alongside events in their life.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Science">
    - Chart the stages of a scientific discovery, from hypothesis through experimentation to publication.
    - Build a geological timeline of Earth's major eras and the life forms associated with each.
    - Map the development of a technology — from early prototype to modern application.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Biography">
    - Create a life events timeline for a historical figure, scientist, author, or artist.
    - Run the personal timeline alongside a world events timeline to show historical context.
    - Let students build their own biography timelines as a class project.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
