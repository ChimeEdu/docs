Flash Cards use spaced repetition — powered by the SM-2 algorithm — to help students build durable, long-term memory. You build the deck once; ChimeEdu handles the science of when each card should reappear. Difficult cards come back sooner, mastered ones less often, so every study session is personalized without any extra work from you or your students.

## How Flash Cards work

Spaced repetition schedules each card based on how well a student knows it. After every review, ChimeEdu records the student's response and adjusts the next review interval accordingly. A card the student answered confidently might not reappear for several days; one they struggled with comes back sooner. The shortest interval is a day, so a card a student gets wrong returns in a later session rather than again in the same one. Over time, this pattern drives cards from short-term recall into long-term memory — the same principle behind decades of learning science research.

## Study modes available to students

Students can switch freely between three study modes depending on how they want to practice. You don't need to configure anything — all three modes are available for every deck you publish.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Flip mode" icon="rotate">
    Students see the term and flip the card to reveal the definition. Quick, low-friction recall practice.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Type the answer" icon="keyboard">

  </Card>

  <Card title="Multiple choice" icon="list-check">

## Creating a Flash Card deck

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New Activity">
    From your classroom dashboard, click **New Activity** and select **Flash Cards** from the activity type list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your cards">
    Add a deck first, then click **Add Card** for each item in it. Every card has a **Front Content** field and a **Back Content** field. Add as many cards as your deck requires. To build a large deck quickly, you can also add many cards at once — see [Import cards from a CSV](#import-cards-from-a-csv) below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add images (optional)">
    On any card, click the image icon to attach a photo or illustration. Images appear on the front or back of the card depending on where you add them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply rich text formatting">
    Use the formatting toolbar to add bold or italic text to any card. Subscript and superscript aren't available, so write scientific and mathematical notation in a form that reads clearly without them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the activity">
    You choose the classroom from a dropdown when you first create the activity, and there is no separate publish step — the deck is available to that classroom as soon as it exists. Everything you add afterwards is live as you save it, so build the deck before sharing the classroom if you would rather students saw it finished.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Import cards from a CSV

Building a large deck one card at a time is slow — especially if your terms already live in a spreadsheet or another study tool. Instead, you can add a whole batch of cards at once by importing a CSV file or pasting rows directly. This is the fastest way to move an existing set — for example, a deck you built in Quizlet — onto ChimeEdu.

Each row becomes one card: the **first column** is the card front (the term), and the **second column** is the back (the definition).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the deck's cards">
    In the flash card editor, expand the deck you want to add to. Next to **Add Card** you'll see an **Import CSV** button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload a file or paste your rows">
    Click **Import CSV**, then either choose a `.csv`, `.tsv`, or `.txt` file, or switch to the **Paste** tab and paste your rows straight from a spreadsheet or another study tool.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the preview">
    ChimeEdu shows exactly what it will create — a table of every card's front and back, plus a count. If your file starts with a header row like "Term, Definition", tick **First row is a header** to skip it. Rows missing a term or a definition are skipped automatically and flagged.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    Click **Import**, and the cards are added to the deck — ready to edit like any other card.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Coming from Quizlet? Export your set (**Export** → comma or tab separated), then upload the file or paste it into the **Paste** tab. Comma, tab, and semicolon separators are all detected automatically, and any term or definition that contains a comma is handled correctly as long as it's quoted — which Quizlet's export does for you.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Imported cards are created as plain text. Add images or bold and italic formatting afterward from each card's editor. You can import up to 1,000 cards at a time — split larger sets across more than one file.
</Note>

## Progress tracking

Students always know where they stand with a deck. ChimeEdu automatically categorizes every card into one of three states, updated in real time as students study:

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Mastered** | The student answered confidently across multiple review sessions. |
| **In progress** | The student has reviewed the card but hasn't yet hit the mastery threshold. |
| **New** | The card hasn't been reviewed yet in this session sequence. |

Progress saves automatically and syncs across devices. A student who studies ten cards on their phone can continue from where they left off on a laptop without any manual sync step.

## Classroom sharing

When you publish a Flash Card deck to a classroom, it appears instantly in the activity list for every enrolled student. You don't need to share a link or send a message — students just open ChimeEdu and the deck is waiting for them. Any updates you make to the deck after publishing are reflected immediately.

<Tip>
  Flash Cards are available any time from any device with a browser. Remind students they can review a deck on their phone during lunch or the night before a test — no app install needed.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Creating and publishing Flash Card decks requires a teacher account. Students need a ChimeEdu account to have their progress saved and synced across devices. Students without accounts can still view a deck but their progress will not be recorded.
</Note>
