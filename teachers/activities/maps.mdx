Map activities anchor knowledge to real places. You set a base map, choose the default view and zoom level, place pins anywhere on the globe, and students explore the content at their own pace — opening pin cards to read descriptions, view images, and build the spatial understanding that a textbook passage simply cannot provide.

## What You Can Do with Maps

<CardGroup cols={2}>

  </Card>

  <Card title="Rich Pin Cards" icon="image">

## Creating a Map Activity

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New Activity">
    From your teacher dashboard, click **New Activity** and select **Map**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the default view">
    Search for a location or drag the map to the area your activity covers. Set the default zoom level so students open the activity with the right geographic context already in view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add pins">
    Click anywhere on the map to drop a pin. Reposition it by dragging, or delete it and start again — pins are fully editable at any time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in each pin card">
    For every pin, add a **title**, a **description** (formatted text is supported), and one or more **images**. The more context you provide, the richer the student experience.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the map">
    The classroom is chosen from a dropdown when you create the activity — there is no separate publish step. The map is available to that classroom straight away, and pins you add or edit are live as you save them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Use the list-view alternative in the activity settings to offer a keyboard- and accessibility-friendly version of the same content. Students who find map navigation difficult can read all pin cards in a structured list without missing any information.
</Tip>

## What Students Experience

When a student opens a Map Activity, they land on the default view you configured. From there they can:

- **Pan and zoom** freely to any part of the map.
- **Click any pin** to open the full pin card with your title, description, and images.
- **Navigate between pins** using the list-view mode for a more linear, accessible experience.

Students move at their own pace. There is no timer and no set order — the activity is designed for independent exploration.

<Note>
  Keyboard navigation between pins works on standard geographic maps: **Tab** reaches each pin, **Enter** opens it, the **arrow keys** pan the map and **\+** / **−** zoom it, and a directional pad in the bottom-left corner does the same with a mouse or touch. On maps that use a custom **image or SVG background**, the pins can't currently be reached by keyboard — for those, the **list view** is the accessible route, and it carries exactly the same pin content. If you build an image-based map, mention list view to your students.
</Note>

<Note>
  Real-world maps, the static map previews on Experience pages, and location search are provided by **Apple Maps**. If your school filters web traffic, ask IT to allow `cdn.apple-mapkit.com` and `*.apple-mapkit.com` — the full list is on the **IT network requirements** page that institution admins can open from the staff dashboard. If those hosts are blocked, the map shows a "could not be loaded on this network" notice with the coordinates, and the **list view** still works, so nothing is lost.
</Note>

## Let students add their own pins

By default students read the pins you place. On a **shared** map you can also let them **add their own pins** — great for collaborative research, an "add a place from the reading" task, or a class-built map that grows over a unit.

Students **enrolled in the linked classroom** just open the map and click **Student Mode** — they can start placing pins right away, **no password needed**. Their pins sit alongside yours, and your pins stay yours to manage.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Link the map to your classroom">
    Student contributions run on classroom enrollment, so the map needs to belong to a classroom with your students in it. See [Enroll Students](/teachers/classrooms/enroll-students).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Students click Student Mode">
    A signed-in, enrolled student opens the map and clicks **Student Mode**, then adds pins using the same title, description, and image fields you use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="(Optional) invite people from outside your class">
    To let students who **aren't** enrolled contribute, open the activity's settings and set a **Student Mode password** under **Access Settings**, then share it with them. They enter it once to unlock editing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The **Student Mode password is only for contributors outside your classroom.** Your enrolled students never need it. If you don't set a password, only your enrolled students — and you — can add pins; everyone else sees a read-only map.
</Note>

<Tip>
  You can change or remove the Student Mode password at any time from **Access Settings** — the panel shows a badge when a password is set. For security it can't be shown back to you, so if you forget it, just set a new one.
</Tip>

<Note>
  This is different from **Per-Student** and **Group** issuance below. Student Mode is one **shared** map that many students add to together; issuance gives each student (or group) their **own** separate copy. Pick shared when you want a single collaborative map, and issuance when you want everyone to build their own.
</Note>

## Give each student their own map

By default a map is one artifact your whole class shares. On Plus and Premium you can issue it **per student** — every student builds their own map, with your pins showing as a locked starting layer — or by **group**, where students placed into groups (A–Z) co-edit one map together. A **Students** panel on the activity page tracks who has started work.

See [Per-Student & Groups](/teachers/activities/issuance) for the full walkthrough.

## Subject Ideas

Not sure where to start? Here are some tried-and-tested uses across the curriculum:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="History" icon="landmark">
    Trace military campaigns, map the expansion of empires, mark the sites of key battles, or chart migration routes across centuries.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Geography" icon="mountain">
    Identify physical features such as mountain ranges, river systems, and tectonic boundaries, or explore human geography topics like population density and urban growth.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Science" icon="flask">
    Plot ecosystems and biomes, map fieldwork data collection sites, track the spread of a species, or visualize climate zones across the globe.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Literature" icon="book">
    Pin the settings of a novel, chart a character's journey, or map the real-world locations that inspired an author's writing.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Modern Studies" icon="newspaper">
    Track the spread of a policy across different countries, map international organizations and their member states, or visualize global data from current events.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Any subject with place" icon="location-dot">
    If your content happens somewhere in the world — at any scale — a Map Activity gives it a location students can explore and remember.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
