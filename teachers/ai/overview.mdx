ChimeEdu includes one optional AI tool: **AI-assisted Study Guide drafting**. You describe what you need, optionally upload source material, and the AI drafts headings and sections into your study guide — which you then edit before students see it.

<Note>
  AI drafting requires a **Premium** account (Administrator and Super Administrator accounts also have access). It is powered by Claude. [View pricing](https://app.chimeedu.com/pricing) to compare plans.
</Note>

Study guides are the only place AI authoring is available. Quizzes, maps, timelines, flash cards, fill-in-the-blank activities, and assessments are all authored manually — there is no AI question generation or blank suggestion on ChimeEdu today.

## How it works in practice

* **It works from your prompt, and from files if you add them.** You can write a prompt on its own ("Create a revision guide on the water cycle for Year 8"), or upload source material for the AI to work from — up to 50,000 characters per file and 500,000 characters in total. Because a prompt alone is enough, the AI can and will generate content that wasn't in any source you provided. Read the draft before you rely on it.
* **The draft lands directly in your study guide.** Generated sections are saved into the guide as they complete — there is no separate "pending draft" you approve first. You can append to existing content, replace it, or regenerate an individual section.
* **Watch out on already-published guides.** If the guide is published and linked to a classroom, saved AI content is visible to students immediately. Draft in an unpublished guide first if you want a review step.
* **There is a usage limit.** Ten generations per hour, per account. Your remaining quota is shown in the AI panel.
* **AI is always optional.** Every study guide can be written, formatted, and published entirely by hand.

## Privacy and your content

The prompt and any files you upload are sent to Anthropic's Claude API to generate your draft. ChimeEdu does **not** keep the material you submit — the usage log records only metadata (the model used, token counts, and whether the request succeeded). The generated content is stored, because it is saved into your study guide by design. Your most recent prompt is also kept in your browser's session storage so the panel can restore it if you reload the page.
