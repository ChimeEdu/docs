Map activities let you explore locations related to what you're studying. Open a pin anywhere on the map to read your teacher's notes and see images. Whether your class is studying ancient civilisations, ecosystems, or the settings of a novel, the map gives every topic a real place in the world.

## How to access a map

Maps are published directly to your classroom feed — you don't need a separate link or login. Head to your classroom dashboard and look for the Map activity in your feed. Click or tap it to open.

## Exploring a map

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the map activity from your classroom">
    Click the Map activity card in your classroom feed. The map will load centered on the area your teacher has set as the starting view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pan around the map">
    Click and drag (or swipe on a touch screen) to move around the map in any direction. You can explore the full geography your teacher has set up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Zoom in and out">
    Use your **scroll wheel** to zoom in and out on a desktop. On a touch device, use a **pinch gesture** to zoom. Zoom right in for street-level detail or pull back to see the bigger picture.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click a pin to open its card">
    You'll see colored pins placed at key locations. Click or tap any pin to open its content card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the pin card">
    Each card contains a **title**, a **description**, and often one or more **images** added by your teacher. Take your time reading — this is where the learning lives.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the card and keep exploring">
    When you're done with a pin, close the card and move on to discover more. You can open as many pins as you like, in any order.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  You don't need to visit pins in a specific order — map activities are designed for free exploration, so go wherever looks interesting first.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Pins all look the same on the map, so there's no way to tell from the outside which ones hold the most detail — it's worth opening each one.
</Tip>

## Adding your own pins

Some maps let you add your own pins, not just read your teacher's. If the map has a **Student Mode** button, you can contribute to it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Student Mode">
    Look for the **Student Mode** button on the map. If you're signed in and enrolled in the class, you can start adding pins right away — no password needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the password if you're asked">
    If your teacher set the map up for students outside the class, you'll be asked for a **map password** first. Your teacher will give it to you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add and fill in your pin">
    Click or tap the map to drop a pin, then add a **title**, a **description**, and any **images** — the same kind of card you read on your teacher's pins.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Not every map allows this. If there's no **Student Mode** button, the map is read-only — just explore and read the pins your teacher placed.
</Note>

## Keyboard and accessibility navigation

You don't have to use a mouse to explore the map. Keyboard navigation works on standard geographic maps, and every map offers a list-view alternative:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Keyboard navigation" icon="keyboard">
    On standard maps, press **Tab** to move between pins, **Enter** to open a pin card, and **Escape** to close it. With the map focused, the **arrow keys** pan it and **\+** / **−** zoom it; the arrow buttons in the bottom-left corner do the same by mouse or touch. If your teacher built the map on a custom image background, the pins can't be reached this way — use list view instead.
  </Card>

  <Card title="List view" icon="list">
    Can't navigate the map easily? Switch to **list view** to browse all pins as a simple, scrollable list — the same content, just in a different format.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

If you use assistive technology or find the map view difficult to navigate, the list-view alternative gives you access to all the same information your teacher has provided.
