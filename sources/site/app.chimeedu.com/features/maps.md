## The world is the classroom.

Geographic context transforms abstract content into something students can orient themselves within. Map activities let you anchor knowledge to real places — so a student studying the spread of the Roman Empire, tracking tectonic plate boundaries, or exploring the trading routes of the Silk Road is always working with the world as it actually exists.

You decide the base map, the zoom level, and exactly which pins are placed and what information they carry. Students explore at their own pace, opening pin cards to read descriptions, view images, and absorb context — all while developing a spatial understanding of the material that a textbook passage simply can't provide.

### Interactive Pins

Place any number of pins anywhere on the map. Each one opens a card with rich content — title, description, images, and optional location details.

### Rich Pin Cards

Each pin card supports formatted text, embedded images, and detailed descriptions. Give every location as much context as it deserves.

### Zoom & Explore

Students pan and zoom freely across the map. The perspective shifts from street-level detail to continental overview — local, regional, or global.

### Any Geography

Set the default view to any location on earth — a single city, a country, a continent, or the whole globe. There are no geographic constraints.

### Works Across Subjects

History, geography, science, literature, current events — any subject with a sense of place benefits from the map format and interactive pins.

### Classroom Published

Maps are shared directly to your classroom feed. Students access them from their dashboard — no separate link, no external platform.
